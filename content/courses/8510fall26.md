courseDescription:
  - "History 8510: Methods in Digital History II is the second course in the digital methods sequence for Digital History PhD students at Clemson University. Where Methods I built foundational computational thinking skills and the practice of turning primary sources into historical datasets, this course takes on advanced methods. Students design and build relational databases, run OCR and AI transcription workflows and measure how accurate they actually are, extract structured data from unstructured sources, apply computer vision and advanced text analysis, and build interactive web applications that function as research tools."
  - "Methods I (HIST 8500) was taught in R. This course is taught in Python and SQL, and the shift is intentional. We have learned to think computationally and while the syntax of these languages will be different, the core skills have not changed. Students work with an AI coding assistant throughout, either Clemson ChatGPT or Claude Code, and the course treats using those tools as a skill in its own right rather than a shortcut around one. The rule for the term is delegate then verify. You may offload big coding tasks to a model, but you are responsible for knowing whether what comes back is correct and for being able to say how you know. You should read all code, verify the results, and generally be the historian in the loop."
  - "Students complete four projects applying these methods to primary sources drawn from their own research, each paired with a short written analysis of the method and its application to a specific historical question. Alongside these, eight Method Logs ask students to work out what each method assumes about a body of sources and whether it has anything to offer their own work. Students leave prepared for original research that uses these techniques to answer complex historical questions, and equally prepared to say when a method is the wrong tool for the question in front of them."

learningObjectives:
  - "Build the technical infrastructure for advanced digital scholarship. Students will design and construct relational databases, build processing pipelines that run reliably at scale, develop interactive web applications using Flask, and maintain reproducible research practices through version control, preparing them for original research and dissertation work that leverages computational approaches."
  - "Evaluate and apply advanced digital methods. Students will critically assess and implement database design, OCR and AI transcription, handwriting recognition, structured data extraction, computer vision, named entity recognition, text reuse detection, embeddings and semantic search, and semantic change detection, choosing among them on the basis of a historical research question rather than novelty."
  - "Establish whether a computational result can be trusted. Students will build ground truth, measure error using appropriate metrics, compare methods against a baseline, and validate samples by hand. Determining that a method does not work on a particular body of sources, and being able to demonstrate why, is a legitimate scholarly outcome in this course."
  - "Practice reproducible research and effective documentation. Students will produce clear documentation of their computational processes, maintain reproducible workflows through GitHub, and present their methodological choices and findings to scholarly audiences."
  - "Work with AI systems as a supervised collaborator. Students will understand the range of technologies gathered under the term artificial intelligence, judge where each is appropriate for historical research, delegate work responsibly and deliberately, and verify what comes back."
