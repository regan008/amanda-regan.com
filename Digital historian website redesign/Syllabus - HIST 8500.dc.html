COURSE DESCRIPTION

This course in computational history teaches you to create, manipulate, explore, and visualize historical data with the goal of advancing historical arguments. We move from the command line and reproducible research, through the fundamentals of programming in R, to a survey of computational methods — visualization, text analysis, topic modeling, and mapping.

Throughout, we ask what historical questions these methods make possible. You'll also build a personal website and portfolio with the static site generator Hugo.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
  • Use GitHub to manage code following reproducible-research best practices.
  • Understand the basic computational patterns shared across programming languages.
  • Create, clean, organize, and document historical data from primary sources.
  • Employ methods like text analysis, topic modeling, and data manipulation.
  • Create static visualizations to communicate your work to other historians.

POLICIES & PROCEDURES
Artificial intelligence (AI) use›

All students in the Digital History Ph.D. program are bound by the program's AI Use Policy (handbook, p. 10); this course aligns with it. In Weeks 1–9, AI tools are strictly prohibited — you must develop foundational computational-thinking skills without AI assistance. Any use before the instructor grants permission results in automatic failure of the assignment and is reported as an academic-integrity violation.

Beginning Week 10, the instructor introduces guardrails. You may then use AI tools to understand code structure, debug, and improve technical skills — but you must understand every line you submit, verify accuracy, and document your AI use in your weekly coding log. AI use is forbidden for historical analysis, interpretation, or writing. Always be the historian in the loop.

Late work›

Because of the technical, scaffolded nature of this course, keeping up with worksheets is essential — miss one and the next is much harder. Unless otherwise stated, assignments are due on the listed date; late work loses 10% per day and is not accepted more than 7 days late.

Academic integrity›

As members of the Clemson community we share a commitment to truthfulness, honor, and responsibility, and do not tolerate lying, cheating, or stealing in any form. See the Graduate Academic Integrity Policy and the current Clemson Graduate Handbook.

Student accessibility services›

Students who experience a barrier to full access should let me know and contact Student Accessibility Services (864-656-6848, Suite 239, Academic Success Center) as early as possible. Send accommodation letters through your AIM portal early each semester.

Conduct, diversity & emergency preparedness›

To learn, we must be open to views different from our own. Please honor the uniqueness of your classmates and refrain from personal attacks. Clemson is committed to equal opportunity and does not discriminate, including the prohibitions mandated by Title IX.

Emergency procedures are posted in all buildings. Sign up for emergency alerts, download the Rave Guardian app, and review guidance from the Clemson Police Department.
